Raw states/sec alone is NOT a reliable progress signal across branches - it depends on
the search strategy in play (a single exhaustive DFS vs IDDFS's repeated shallow restarts
vs a pruning check that cuts off whole subtrees before they're even generated/hashed), not
just how fast one state can be processed. The metric that actually tracks the point of this
project - solving cubes faster - is solve time:
  bmark-1: only 4-5/10 size-5 scrambles solved, average ~1800-4500ms among successes
           (many of the rest timed out at the 60s safety cap without finding a solution).
  bmark-2: 6-7/10 solved, average ~7-12ms - both more reliable AND roughly 2-3 orders of
           magnitude faster, despite exploring FEWER raw states per second than bmark-1 in
           the states-explored benchmark. IDDFS finds a shallow solution the moment one
           depth level reveals it, instead of exhaustively searching one fixed depth that
           may be far deeper than necessary (or, worse, insufficient for a given scramble).
  bmark-7 (this branch): size-5 scrambles solve in ~0ms average, and the solver now scales
           to reliably solving scrambles up to size 30 while staying under a ~10s average -
           a regime bmark-1 could not even reach for size 5.

Read together with the depth stats above: bmark-3's pruning and bmark-5/6's search-path
pruning both cut average states/sec (because a meaningful fraction of branching is skipped
before it ever becomes a hashed/visited state), yet those same branches reach much deeper
max search depth in the same 10s window than bmark-1/bmark-2 (up to 20-21 vs 20 hit via a
different exploration pattern) - the search is doing less wasted work per unit of real
progress, which is exactly what the solve-time numbers above confirm directly.
