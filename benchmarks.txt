  bmark-3 non-domino: 2304999 states, max depth 21, 230109 states/sec (breadth ~= 109762 states/depth-level; total_depth_searched not recorded in bmark-3)

  bmark-4 domino:     17274000 states, max depth 11, 1.7274e+06 states/sec (avg depth/state 10.0923; breadth ~= 1.57036e+06 states/depth-level)

  On this run, bmark-4's raw states/sec is actually HIGHER than bmark-3's, not lower --
  so any earlier read of bmark-4 being slower in states/sec does not hold on this machine/run.
  What stands out instead is depth reached: bmark-4 tops out at max depth 7 (non-domino) /
  11 (domino) versus bmark-3's 21/21 in the same time budget, even though bmark-4 explores
  far MORE total states. The search-breadth figures explain why: bmark-4's states-per-depth-
  level is roughly 15-19x bmark-3's. Since explore_benchmark() re-runs a full DFS with a fresh
  `visited` set for every outer max_depth (both branches use the same search_depth=20 cap and
  the same banned_next_moves pruning), that gap means bmark-4's visited-state dedup is
  collapsing far fewer states at a given nominal depth than bmark-3's vector-based
  representation does -- so the 10s budget is consumed inside one shallow max_depth iteration
  instead of progressing through many. We cannot confirm from this benchmark alone whether
  that is a real difference in how many physically-distinct cube states exist at that depth,
  or a sign that rotation.h's bit-shift move/cycle logic still fails to canonicalise some
  states to the same bit pattern that the vector representation would treat as identical --
  this commit's own message ("Fixed all move bugs I think") is explicitly uncertain, so a
  residual move bug is plausible but not proven here.
  Either way, raw states/sec is only one proxy for whether this branch's bit-shift/long state
  representation was a successful optimisation. This benchmark's actual intent per the bmark-4
  commit was correctness of the bit-shift representation, not exploration throughput -- the
  real payoff of two `long`s over two `vector<pair<int,int>>`s (no per-move heap allocation)
  shows up in memory/allocation overhead during real solves, which this synthetic exploration
  benchmark does not directly measure.
